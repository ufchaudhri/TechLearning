The 6-Module Leadership Track

Six modules mapped directly to what you asked for: concepts, system-design issues, end-to-end architecture, governance, value measurement, and value creation. At 3–5 hrs/week, take one module every 1–2 weeks (~6–10 weeks total). Each expands to a set of executive actions and ends in an artifact you can actually put in front of your leadership team or board.

How this differs from an IC track: you're not learning to build — you're learning to judge, govern, and fund. The goal of each concept is a better question to ask your team and a better decision at the table. Depth is calibrated to "can interrogate a design review," not "can write the code."

Learning System — retention for executives

Executives retain what they use and what they teach. This system is built around applying every concept to a live decision in your org and pressure-testing it out loud — not passive study.

The weekly executive loop (≈3–5 hrs): 1) Learn one concept (60–90 min). 2) Map it to a real decision or project on your plate right now. 3) Draft one paragraph of the module's artifact. 4) Pressure-test: raise it in a 1:1 with an engineer/architect, or explain it to a peer. Teaching is the retention mechanism.

1 · Apply to a live decision

Abstract AI knowledge fades; a concept attached to a real bet sticks. Anchor each module to something already in your portfolio.

  • Keep a running "decisions this changes" note per module.
  • Turn each concept into a question you'll ask in your next review.

2 · Teach it up and across

Explaining to your leadership team or board forces clarity and exposes gaps — and builds your reputation as the AI-fluent leader.

  • Run a 20-min "AI concept of the week" in your staff meeting.
  • If you can't explain the tradeoff simply, you don't own it yet.

3 · Spaced review of the essentials

Revisit the handful of frameworks you'll actually deploy on an expanding schedule so they're recall-ready in the room.

4 · Build an expert bench

Leaders scale learning through people. Curate a small circle you can pressure-test thinking against.

  • Identify 2–3 internal experts (architect, ML lead, security/legal).
  • Book a recurring 30-min "AI office hours" to stress-test decisions.
  • Keep a decision journal: what you decided, why, and what you'd revisit.

    The board narrative

    Only ~29% of executives say they can measure AI ROI with confidence. Being the leader who can is a differentiator.

    • Lead with the four value channels: cost, revenue, risk reduction, strategic optionality.
    • Always show the baseline — most ROI disputes trace to a missing baseline, not failed tech.

    Govern with confidence

    • Adopt NIST AI RMF as the operating model, ISO 42001 as the certifiable system, and map EU AI Act exposure.
    • Name owners: who signs off on risk tier, evals, incidents.
    • Model cards + a risk register turn governance from slideware into practice.

    Interrogate any AI project

    • Ask: what's the eval? what's the failure mode? what's cost-per-outcome?
    • Build-vs-buy: where is the durable advantage — model, data, or workflow?
    • Demand a monitoring + rollback plan before scaling.

    Position yourself

    • Reframe your track record as "AI-governed delivery at scale."
    • Sponsor one visible, measurable AI win with a clean before/after.
    • Publish a POV (internal memo or talk) — leadership is demonstrated in the open.

    How to run this track

    • Cadence: one module every 1–2 weeks at 3–5 hrs — realistic for an SVP calendar. Front-load Modules 1–3 (literacy + design + architecture); they make 4–6 sharper.
    • Every module ships an artifact. Draft it as you learn; the artifact is the proof and the deliverable, not a side task.
    • Use your real org as the case study. Apply each framework to a live project — that's where retention and impact come from.
