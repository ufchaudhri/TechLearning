Gen AI Concepts for Decision-Makers

A one-page orientation for leaders — what Gen AI is, how it works end-to-end, where it breaks, and how we govern and measure it. Built on the Why / What / How model.

WHY

Why this matters now

Modern Gen AI turns unstructured language and knowledge work into something we can automate, augment, and scale.

  • Efficiency: compress cost and cycle time on knowledge tasks.
  • Growth: new products and experiences not previously feasible.
  • Risk of inaction: competitors compound advantage while we wait.
WHAT

What it actually is

A large language model (LLM) is a statistical engine that predicts text. It doesn't "know" facts — it generates plausible output from patterns.

  • Powerful at language, synthesis, and reasoning-like tasks.
  • Not a database and not deterministic — it must be engineered into a reliable system.
HOW

How we make it useful

Value comes from the system around the model, not the model alone: grounding it in our data, orchestrating steps, adding guardrails, and measuring output.

  • Reference architecture below shows each layer + example tech.
  • Governance, LLMOps, and value measurement wrap the whole thing.

Key concepts in one line each

Tokens & context window: the chunks a model reads/writes; the window caps how much it can consider at once.
Embeddings: numeric meaning-vectors that power semantic search and retrieval.
RAG (retrieval-augmented generation): fetch relevant company data, then have the model answer grounded in it.
Fine-tuning: adapting a model's weights to a narrow task/style when prompting + RAG aren't enough.
Agents & tools: models that plan multi-step work and call tools/APIs to act, not just answer.
Evals: test suites that score output quality — the difference between "demos well" and "works."

Risk & Governance

Three complementary anchors — most enterprises run two or more together:

  • NIST AI RMF — operational risk-management method (Govern / Map / Measure / Manage).
  • ISO/IEC 42001 — certifiable AI management system; increasingly a procurement requirement.
  • EU AI Act — law, by risk tier; applies to anyone deploying AI in EU markets.

Operating model essentials: named owners for risk sign-off, risk-tiered use cases, model cards, a Gen AI risk register, and an incident-response + rollback plan.

LLMOps

The discipline that turns a demo into a production system you can debug, improve, and trust.

  • Offline eval: score against golden datasets in CI — gates releases like unit tests.
  • Online eval: score sampled live traffic; alert on quality regression/drift.
  • Observability: trace every step (prompt, retrieval, tool, cost, latency) end to end.
  • Prompt/version management so changes are controlled, not ad hoc.

Tools: Langfuse, Arize Phoenix, LangSmith, Future AGI (OpenTelemetry-based).

How we measure value

Capture a baseline first. The #1 reason ROI can't be proven is that no before-state was measured — not that the tech failed.

Three-tier ROI (so you don't abandon early):

  • Realized — hard dollars already in the P&L.
  • Trending — leading indicators pointing at future return.
  • Capability — durable new abilities/optionality.

Four value channels (most business cases only address the first):

  • Cost reduction · Revenue contribution · Risk reduction · Strategic optionality.

Best metric: cost per outcome — fully-loaded cost per completed unit of work, before vs after Gen AI — beats "hours saved," which rarely reaches the operating statement.

Dashboard: ~5 KPIs tied to efficiency, risk, and financial impact.
