Methodology · Build Sequence

Six-Phase Build Framework

Every engagement — regardless of domain, scope, or timeline — follows this sequence. Phases 1–3 can be compressed into a single Claude conversation using the Kickoff Prompt. Nothing in this process blocks on domain expertise you don't yet have.

Output: a structured domain brief covering key personas, primary workflows, canonical data entities, industry KPIs, relevant standards or compliance context (ISO, PCI, HIPAA, etc.), and the vocabulary used by practitioners in that space. This brief becomes the source of truth for all subsequent phases.

Core Principle
Domain expertise is researched, not assumed. At any phase, saying "I don't know — take the lead" is a valid and correct response. Claude documents every assumption explicitly, and all assumptions are reviewable before delivery. The prototype is only as weak as the assumptions that were left unreviewed.
Phase 02 · Scoping Interview

Discovery Questions

These questions structure every scoping conversation. Claude works through them in groups. For each question you cannot answer, Claude states its assumption using the fallback shown below the card. All assumptions feed the Assumptions Log.

Group A — Domain & Audience

A.1
What industry or domain is this for?
The specific vertical — payments, supply chain, healthcare data, HR tech, fintech, logistics, observability, risk management, etc.
Claude researches 2–3 candidate domains and selects the one with the richest data model and strongest executive narrative for demonstration.
A.2
Who is the primary end user of the product?
Job title, seniority level, technical depth, and the specific daily workflow pain point this product relieves.
Claude defines a realistic persona at VP or Director level based on domain research, including a named role, responsibilities, and top-3 frustrations.
A.3
What is the product's core value proposition?
The one sentence that explains why someone would pay for this — the problem eliminated or the outcome delivered.
Claude infers the strongest value prop from domain research, competitive landscape, and the persona's identified pain points.
A.4
Who is the demo audience?
The specific role, seniority, and company type of the person who will see this demo — shapes depth, vocabulary, and what to emphasise.
Claude defaults to a VP-level technical buyer (CDO, CTO, or Head of Engineering) at a Series B company in the identified vertical.

Group B — Functional Scope

B.1
What are the 2–3 core screens?
The views the demo will navigate through — dashboard, drill-down, alert detail, configuration, approval queue, etc.
Claude designs the canonical screen set for the domain, confirmed against competitor research and common SaaS patterns in the vertical.
B.2
What is the primary interactive action?
The single most impressive thing a user can do in the demo: filter, drill down, approve, configure, generate an alert, chat with an AI assistant.
Claude selects the interaction that most effectively demonstrates intelligence and operational value for the target buyer.
B.3
What KPIs or metrics must appear?
The specific numbers that prove the product works — throughput, SLO compliance, error rate, revenue at risk, processing latency, data quality score, etc.
Claude uses the canonical KPI framework for the domain (e.g. RED/USE for observability, DQ dimensions for data quality, FTE savings for automation).
B.4
What data volume and scale looks credible?
Approximate transaction counts, record volumes, or event rates that match the target company's scale — too few looks like a toy, too many looks implausible.
Claude benchmarks to a Series B company in the vertical using publicly available industry data and generates volumes accordingly.

Group C — Branding & Constraints

C.1
Is there a product or company name to use?
The name displayed in the prototype header. Can be the client's actual product name, a white-label name, or a fictional placeholder.
Claude generates a credible SaaS product name appropriate to the domain and buyer — professional, not generic.
C.2
Any visual style or colour preferences?
Light vs dark theme, conservative vs modern, existing brand colours or hex codes, any competitor UI to reference or differentiate from.
Claude selects a dark, refined aesthetic with a gold accent — a combination that reads as premium and technically sophisticated to executive buyers.
C.3
What must the prototype NOT show or imply?
Features under NDA, competitor comparisons, pricing signals, capabilities not yet built, real customer data, or anything that creates a legal or reputational risk.
Claude applies demo-safe defaults: no pricing, no named competitors, no real PII, no uncommitted product roadmap features.
C.4
What is the success definition for this demo?
The specific reaction, outcome, or next step the demo is designed to trigger — a follow-up meeting, a pilot agreement, a procurement conversation, a technical deep-dive.
Claude optimises the prototype to generate a clear "I want to see more" reaction — compelling enough to advance the conversation, not so complete it eliminates the need for one.
Assumption Protocol
For every question you cannot answer, respond with: "I don't know — please assume and document." Claude will state the assumption in one sentence, confirm it is reasonable, add it to the Assumptions Log with a rationale, and proceed. You review the log before any client interaction. Undisclosed assumptions are the primary risk in prototype demos — this protocol eliminates that risk.
Phase 03 · Input Materials

Input Artifact Inventory

Provide whatever materials you have. Claude classifies each against the quality tiers below and generates realistic synthetic replacements for anything missing. Having nothing is manageable — the Bronze tier still produces a credible, demo-ready prototype.

📄
Sample Data Files
CSV, JSON, or Excel exports from any existing system. Claude extracts the schema, field names, and value patterns, then generates a synthetic dataset at the right scale.
Transaction logs, customer records, event streams, audit trails, API response payloads
🖼️
UI Screenshots
Screenshots of existing tools, competitor products, or rough sketches. Used for layout reference, feature parity analysis, and visual differentiation strategy.
Current dashboard, Figma mockup, Miro board, hand sketch photograph
📋
Process Documents
SOPs, runbooks, workflow diagrams, and operating procedures. Claude extracts the real steps users follow and builds them into the prototype flow and navigation.
Incident runbooks, approval workflows, onboarding guides, operating procedures
📊
Reports & Decks
Existing presentations, QBRs, or board decks. Used to extract real metrics, executive vocabulary, and the narrative the prototype should reinforce.
Quarterly business reviews, board decks, audit reports, investor materials

Architecture Diagrams
System architecture, data flow maps, or integration topology. Grounds the prototype in a technically credible system model that withstands CTO scrutiny.
AWS architecture, Miro system map, draw.io export, Lucidchart, C4 diagram
📝
API / Schema Specs
OpenAPI specs, data dictionaries, or database schemas. Ensures all field names, data types, and entity relationships are technically accurate.
Swagger YAML, ERD, data dictionary, ISO message specifications (pacs.008, camt.054)
🧑‍💼
Persona Descriptions
Job descriptions, LinkedIn profiles, or org charts for the target user or buyer role. Ensures terminology, pain points, and workflow language feel authentic.
Job descriptions, org charts, LinkedIn role summaries, buyer persona documents
📰
Industry Research
Analyst reports, white papers, or trade publications. Provides benchmark metrics, industry vocabulary, and the macro trends the prototype can reference.
Gartner reports, McKinsey briefs, IDC studies, industry association publications
🔗
Reference URLs
Competitor product pages, inspiration references, or documentation sites. Claude researches the product's feature set, UI patterns, and positioning claims.
Competitor product pages, feature comparison pages, public API documentation

Quality Tier Assessment

Gold
Real data + architecture + persona document
Maximum realism. Claude produces a prototype indistinguishable from a production product in terms of data fidelity, technical depth, and user language. Recommended for late-stage advisory conversations where technical credibility is being directly evaluated.
Silver
Screenshots or one sample data file
Good realism. Claude reverse-engineers the schema and visual language, then fills in the rest synthetically. Sufficient for most executive demos where the audience is evaluating capability, not auditing data.
Bronze
Domain name only — no materials available
Claude researches the domain from scratch using web research and industry benchmarks. Produces a credible prototype with correct vocabulary and plausible data. Appropriate for first-contact advisory conversations and unsolicited capability demonstrations.
Zero-Input Prompt
If you have no input materials, tell Claude: "I have no input artifacts. Research the [domain] space and generate a complete synthetic dataset including realistic field names, plausible volumes, and edge cases typical of a Series B company in this vertical. Use the Bronze tier approach and document all assumptions." Claude will produce everything needed to proceed.

Layout Rules

R1
Metric cards anchor every dashboard
Every dashboard view opens with 3–5 KPI cards in a horizontal grid. Each card: 12px uppercase label, 24–28px primary value (weight 500), and a delta indicator with directional colour (green = up good, red = up bad as appropriate for the metric).
Structure: [LABEL] / [VALUE] / [±DELTA %] — never omit the delta
R2
One primary chart per screen
Time-series line, funnel, distribution, or heatmap — chosen specifically to support the story the KPI cards are telling. No decorative, redundant, or "dashboard bloat" charts. Every chart must earn its space by answering a question the KPIs raise.
R3
Sortable, filterable data table below the fold
Column headers sortable on click. At minimum one filter (status or time range). Row-level actions visible on hover. Status badges using the colour system. All technical identifiers in monospace. Timestamps in ISO 8601 or the domain-standard format.
R4
AI Insights panel on every dashboard view
Purple-accented right panel or collapsible drawer. Contains exactly three elements: one headline insight (what is happening), one recommendation (what to do), one active alert (what to watch). Signals intelligence without crowding the operational view. Clearly labeled as AI-generated.
R5
Zero placeholder text — absolute rule
Lorem ipsum, "Sample data", "Coming soon", "N/A", "TBD", "Example Company", "User Name" — banned without exception. Every visible string must use domain-accurate vocabulary and realistic values. Violated by any Claude generation? Instruct a rebuild of the offending component.
R6
Spacing scale: 4 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 24 / 32 / 48px
Internal component padding: 12–16px. Card-to-card gap: 12px. Section-to-section gap: 24–32px. Page margin: 32–48px. No arbitrary pixel values outside this scale anywhere in the prototype.
R7
Meaningful interaction on the first click
A filter activates, a drill-down opens, a modal appears, or a visible state changes on the first interaction. A prototype requiring 3+ clicks to demonstrate any interactivity fails the demo standard. Every control must do something visible and non-trivial.
R8
Polished at 1280px — the presentation viewport
Prototypes must render without horizontal overflow, collapsed grids, or truncated labels at 1280×800 — the standard laptop screen in presentation mode. Test in a browser at this resolution before any client interaction.
Engagement Management · Risk & Quality

Governance Framework

The framework that makes this playbook robust enough for fractional CDO engagements. Covers the Assumptions Log protocol, demo risk management, quality gates, and intellectual property hygiene.

RACI — Roles & Responsibilities

You — Fractional CDO
  • Approve domain brief before build begins
  • Review and sign off Assumptions Log
  • Conduct client handoff checklist
  • Own all client communications referencing the prototype
  • Decide what is and is not shown in a live demo
  • Flag any real client data before Claude sees it
Claude — Research & Build
  • Research domain and produce the brief
  • Conduct the scoping interview
  • Generate and classify synthetic data
  • Build the prototype per UI standards
  • Maintain and update the Assumptions Log
  • Produce the documentation package
Client — Prospect / Stakeholder
  • Receives the shareable documentation URL
  • Interacts with the live prototype
  • Reviews the Assumptions Log tab
  • Does not see Claude conversation history
  • Evaluates capability, not production code
Shared — Both Parties
  • No real client PII ever enters a prototype
  • No real proprietary systems are referenced by name without consent
  • Prototype is clearly labeled as a demonstration artifact
  • Assumptions Log is disclosed before any commercial discussion

Assumptions Log Protocol

Open
Assumption stated, not yet reviewed
Claude has made an assumption and documented it. You have not yet confirmed or overridden it. The prototype is not client-ready while Open assumptions exist for high-stakes claims.
Confirmed
You have reviewed and accepted the assumption
You have read the assumption, agree it is reasonable, and accept responsibility for it in client conversations. Confirmed assumptions can be disclosed proactively as a sign of rigor.
Override
You have replaced the assumption with a verified fact
You provided the correct information, Claude has updated the prototype and documentation, and the original assumption is logged as superseded. This is the highest-quality state.

Quality Gates — Do Not Proceed Without These

  • Gate 1 (after Phase 2): Domain brief reviewed and approved. No material inaccuracies in terminology, personas, or KPIs.
  • Gate 2 (after Phase 2): Assumptions Log reviewed. All Open assumptions either confirmed or overridden before build begins.
  • Gate 3 (after Phase 4): Wireframe approved. Screen inventory, information hierarchy, and user journey confirmed in writing.
  • Gate 4 (after Phase 5): Zero placeholder text confirmed. All interactive elements tested. Primary user journey runs in under 60 seconds.
  • Gate 5 (after Phase 6): Documentation package reviewed. Assumptions Log tab is accurate. Prototype labeled as demonstration artifact.
  • Gate 6 (pre-client): Tested at 1280×800 in a fresh browser tab. Shared URL opens correctly without login. Screen recording made as backup.
Hard Stop — Never Do These
Never use real client data, real customer PII, or real transaction records from any current or former employer. Never reference a real named client, account, or counterparty without explicit consent. Never present the prototype as production-ready software. Never share the GitHub repository URL with a client — share only the GitHub Pages URL or a Netlify link. Never demo from a laptop without first running the handoff checklist.
Phase 06 · Client Deliverable

Documentation Package

Every prototype ships with a companion documentation artifact. Built as a standalone HTML file with its own tab navigation — clients receive one URL that covers the live prototype, a guided walkthrough, the domain research, and the full assumptions log.

Required Tab Structure

01Overview
Business problem statement, solution narrative, and value proposition in 200–300 words. Executive tone — written for a CDO or VP Engineering encountering the prototype for the first time. No technical jargon. This is the tab that earns the next conversation.
02How to Use
Numbered walkthrough of every interactive element, with a suggested 1–2 sentence demo script per step. Functions as a rehearsal guide for the presenter and a self-service guide for clients exploring independently. Written as a tour, not a manual.
03Domain Context
The full domain research brief: personas with named roles and frustrations, workflow descriptions, data model with key entities and fields, KPIs with industry benchmarks, and relevant standards or compliance context. Proves the prototype reflects real operational reality.
04Design Decisions
UI choices and their rationale — why dark theme, why this colour system, why these components. Typographic hierarchy explained. Accessibility considerations noted. AI Insights panel design rationale. Demonstrates engineering discipline and design intentionality.
05Assumptions Log
Every assumption made during the engagement: the assumption statement, the rationale, and its status (Confirmed / Open / Override). Formatted as a numbered, scannable list. The transparency layer that converts scrutiny into trust — the best clients appreciate seeing it.
06Technical Notes
Stack, synthetic data methodology, architecture model referenced, data entity relationships, and extension points for production implementation. Includes a rough effort estimate (T-shirt size) to move from prototype to MVP. Credibility layer for CTO and solutions architect audiences.

Client Handoff Checklist

  • Replace all placeholder company or product names with the client's name or an approved fictional brand
  • Verify every metric value is plausible at the client's company scale — no round numbers, no default values
  • Review the Assumptions Log tab — every entry is accurate, confirmed, or overridden
  • Confirm the "Prototype — Demonstration Artifact" label is visible in the footer
  • Test all interactive elements in a fresh browser tab at 1280×800 resolution
  • Confirm zero placeholder text remains in any screen ("Lorem", "Sample", "N/A", "TBD", "Example")
  • Confirm the primary user journey completes in under 60 seconds without instruction
  • Verify the shared URL is accessible without a Claude.ai or GitHub login
